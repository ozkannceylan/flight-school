Discrete Bayes: predict with a motion kernel, update by $\mathrm{bel}\odot p(z\mid x)$, then normalise.

$p(z=\mathrm{door}\mid \mathrm{door})=0.9$, $p(z=\mathrm{door}\mid \mathrm{empty})=0.2$. Swap those two numbers (`inverted_likelihood`) and the same eight observations make a peaked, *wrong* posterior. A normalised belief is not a true pose. Lab 23 will ask you to cite `labs/lab11_bayes/check.py`.
